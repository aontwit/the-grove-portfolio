## Q1. What process does impeccable prescribe (phases, direction contracts, comps, critique loops, audits), and what separates "AI slop" from production quality?

### Takeaway
Impeccable treats production quality as the output of a gated pipeline, not a prompt: write product truth down first (PRODUCT.md), commit to one specific "world" instead of the category default (direction contract), then either build from an approved image comp that is measured region by region or build code-led with the ambition written into the contract. After that come a capped critique loop, an independent finish review with a four-word verdict, and DESIGN.md written from what actually shipped. The site says slop is the convergent category default ("if someone could guess your aesthetic from the category alone… rework"), plus invented claims, decorative chrome standing in for authored content, and browser defaults left unthemed.

### Cited Findings

**Top-level modes (website)**
- Four modes: **Start** ("Turn an idea into a designed page"), **Improve** ("Make an existing interface work better"), **Check** ("Find what needs attention before release"), **Maintain** ("Keep the design consistent as it grows"). — [Designing](https://impeccable.style/designing/)
- Context files: `PRODUCT.md` records "audience, purpose, and constraints"; `DESIGN.md` records the visual system separately; existing code also counts as context. Habits: "Give it one clear set of design instructions", "Correct stale product facts", "Keep your own judgment… your goals, constraints, and feedback guide the result", and "expect a few rounds of prompting and review." — [Designing](https://impeccable.style/designing/)
- Each command's job: `/shape` ("agree on a plan and stop before implementation"), `/layout` `/typeset` `/colorize` (targeted edits), `/critique` (diagnostic review), `/generate` (variants of one element), `/audit` ("accessibility, performance, responsive behavior, theming, and anti-patterns"), `/clarify` ("labels, instructions, errors, and empty states"), `/harden` ("missing data, long content, failures, and recovery"), `/polish` ("hierarchy, spacing, consistency, and interaction"), `/extract`, `/document`, and `/hooks` ("Run automated detector checks after relevant agent edits"). — [Designing](https://impeccable.style/designing/)
- Example of a constraint the site asks for: "Use confirmed dates and capacity. Do not invent availability." — [Designing](https://impeccable.style/designing/)

**New-work flow: Describe the job → Choose a direction → Build and review**
- The direction record holds "the central idea, visual language, visitor's path, first-screen composition, chosen form and seed, and what must be checked before the work is complete." — [Docs: new work](https://impeccable.style/docs/new-work)
- Two paths. **Mockup-first (comp-led)** gives "bolder compositions", but "image-to-code translation loses detail" and it takes several rounds. **Code-first** gives a "stronger first implementation within the model's natural range", faster but visually more conservative. — [Docs: new work](https://impeccable.style/docs/new-work); the [Designing page](https://impeccable.style/designing/) adds that code-led "tends to look more coherent on the first pass."
- Five gates every direction must pass before users see it: **Truth** (claims and data accurate), **Purpose** (structure lets the task get done), **Consequence** (distinctive elements improve the experience, not just decorate), **Feasibility** (works across devices with the assets at hand), **Fit** (trade-offs respect the brief). — [Docs: new work](https://impeccable.style/docs/new-work)
- What gets saved: an updated `DESIGN.md`, surface briefs in `.impeccable/surfaces/`, and mockups in `.impeccable/mocks/`. — [Docs: new work](https://impeccable.style/docs/new-work)

**Detailed mechanics (local skill `reference/new-work.md`, shipped copy of the GitHub repo)**
- Visitor modes drive everything. **Persuade**: who must act, what they should believe, which real proof earns that belief. **Operate**, **Read** and **Experience** are the others. The skill says to ask "what would make a polished result feel wrong", and "Never ask for CSS values or canned aesthetic lanes." — local `reference/new-work.md` (repo: https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)
- How a new world is found: name the product's unique mechanism in one sentence, the audience's real scene and cultural home, and what the first surface must prove. Note "the page this category always ships and its predictable opposite"; both count as "the rut". Then list **seven concrete visual systems, artifacts, places or rituals the audience knows by heart**, spanning at least three material families ("What would this thing look like as a physical object; what did its world look like before the web?"). — local `reference/new-work.md`
- A `concept-seed` script "rolls" which direction to build, so runs don't converge on the default. "The roll is what keeps every run from converging on the category default." Each option is presented as a card with thesis, palette, materials, first viewport and honest risk. A plain "category standard" exit is always offered, and when the user picks it the bar becomes "two or three products this should sit alongside… execute the canon at full fidelity." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Direction contract.** Six short blocks, about 150 words, written before any code: **THESIS** (the one idea this surface owns and the category-default arrangement it refuses), **OWN-WORLD** (palette and component language "specific enough to be recognizable with all content removed"), **STORY** (what the visitor understands, believes, does), **FIRST VIEWPORT** (exact composition, scale, where the primary action sits), **FORM**, and **FINISH**: "unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished." Rule: "If a block reads like a mood, the direction is not decided yet." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- Color strategy is chosen before colors: Restrained (neutrals plus one accent), Committed (one saturated color on 30–60% of the surface), Full palette (3–4 named roles) or Drenched. "Dark or light is never a default: write one sentence of physical scene (who uses this, where, under what light) and let it force the answer." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- Training-default fonts are named as "you stopped looking": Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Syne, Space Grotesk, Space Mono, IBM Plex, Inter-as-display, DM Sans/Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans. Picking one needs a reason "no other face could satisfy." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Calibration (the definition of slop).** "AI-generated interfaces cluster around a few looks regardless of subject: warm cream ground, high-contrast serif display, and a terracotta or signal-red accent; near-black with one neon accent and glowing edges; broadsheet-editorial hairlines, italic display serif, and small tracked mono labels… if someone could guess your aesthetic from the category alone… rework." It also names the model's own bias: "warm, bookish, family… subjects come out as cream grounds, serif display with italic accents, and lamplight… Treat that first palette as already spent." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Comp-led build as a gated state machine.** (0) three comps, the user approves one; (1) **spec**: grid over the comp, every region named by kind (plate/image/texture vs text/control/chrome), type *measured* with `font-match` against Google Fonts; (2) **plates**: every painted or photographic region regenerated as a real asset at ≥1.5× size ("A crop of the comp is never a plate"; "Anything drawn is a plate", meaning no inline-SVG imitation of artwork); (3) **hero**: build the first viewport only and score it with `comp-diff`; passing needs **72% overall** with no hard veto; (4) sections; (5) motion; (6) responsive at 1280–1600 and 390. The comp is "a spatial contract, not a mood board," because "Models systematically believe their HTML, CSS, and SVG recreation of an image succeeded when it did not." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Rules for both paths.** "The first viewport is a thesis, not a header… The memory test: if someone left after one viewport, what would they describe an hour later? If the honest answer is a mood, the concept has not committed yet." / "Prove, don't claim… Demonstration data is design material: author it at full fidelity and label it synthetic; claims stay uninventable." / "Author the assets; never substitute chrome… Gradients, glass, generic icon tiles… are the gap wearing chrome." / "Pace the scroll like a studio… a dense passage earns a quiet one, and the page ends anchored by a real close." / "Author motion as material… orchestrated, rather than scattered hover effects." / "Use real, verified imagery… one decisive photo beats five mediocre ones." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Truth rule.** Illustrative material is fine if labelled; "What stays uninventable are commercial and factual claims: prices, customers, benchmarks…" and "Refusing a bold direction because its demonstration data does not exist yet is the timidity reflex wearing honesty's clothes." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Inspect and finish.** Capture desktop and mobile in one batched round, critique against the contract, and stop at **two rounds**. Run the `detect` anti-pattern scanner once. Then a *fresh* independent **finish reviewer** (no shared transcript, "a reviewer that inherits your transcript inherits your framing, your optimism") returns exactly one disposition: **recapture / rebuild / fix / ship**. After that, a documenter writes DESIGN.md "from the built world," because "a rulebook written before the build gets defended against reality." — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **What the finish reviewer checks.** Evidence validity, persistence, fidelity matrix (mandatory **TYPE / MATERIAL / GROUND** rows; "faked physicality (CSS bevels, embossing… chalk effects imitating a material the page never renders) as contradicted… imitation material is the single most reliable mark of machine-made design"; ground drift "toward the rendition prior (warm cream on light grounds, blue-black slate on dark)"), ceiling against the world's QUALITY BAR card, the contract promise by promise including the memory test, truth, and the floor (Refuse list). Disposition is "derived, never felt"; "A page a design director would send back is fix at best however functional it is." — local `C:\Users\usEr\.claude\agents\impeccable-finish-reviewer.md`
- **Craft floor ("Verify").** Contrast ≥4.5:1 body / ≥3:1 large; shadows with offset plus soft blur; "more space above a heading than below it"; body measure 65–75ch, display max 6rem, tracking floor −0.04em; "one authored moment" of motion with exponential ease-out from an already-visible default; hover, disabled, loading, error and empty states; **"Browser surfaces"**, meaning theming text selection, caret, scrollbars, focus rings, underline offset and tabular numerals, "the cheapest signal that a page was built rather than assembled, and the one models skip most reliably." — local `reference/craft-floor.md`

**Critique / audit (website docs)**
- Critique covers design quality (intentionality, genericity, consistency), usability scored 0–4 (n/a items drop out of the total) and perspective walkthroughs. It combines "a design review with automated checks for known anti-patterns" and saves reports to `.impeccable/critique/`. — [Docs: critique](https://impeccable.style/docs/critique)
- Audit scores five web dimensions 0–4 each (total /20): Accessibility, Performance, Theming, Responsive behaviour and Implementation integrity. Severity runs P0 Blocking → P1 Major → P2 Minor → P3 Polish. "A strong total can still contain a blocking issue"; it "leaves the code unchanged." — [Docs: audit](https://impeccable.style/docs/audit)
- The Neo Mirai case study's path is brand toolkit → page mockup → working site. The team "carr[ied] over the mockup's large opening artwork" and "regenerat[ed] or separat[ed] images into assets the page could use." Browser passes fixed "overlaps, awkward image crops, navigation states, and speaker carousel issues." — [Case: Neo Mirai](https://impeccable.style/cases/neo-mirai)

### Inferences
- The core idea is commitment plus verification. One specific world is chosen against a named rut. Its promises are written down (contract). The render is then checked against those promises by someone other than the builder, with a cap on fix rounds.
- "Production-grade" here means: true (no invented facts), complete (every state, every viewport, themed browser surfaces), authored (real assets, not CSS stand-ins), and specific (could not be mistaken for another product in the same category).

### Gaps
- The website's /designing page summarizes the process. The detailed rules (seven candidates, 72% hero gate, TYPE/MATERIAL/GROUND rows) come from the locally installed skill files, not from a public web page I fetched. The GitHub repo is linked from the site but I did not fetch the repo pages themselves.
- No quantitative evidence (e.g., user testing) that the process improves conversion. Evidence is the curated gallery.

## Q2. What traits do the luxury/boutique-hotel gallery examples (Opus 5.5) share?

### Takeaway
All runs got the same brief: Villa Castagneto, a 14-room family hotel on a Ligurian cliff, audience couples aged 40–60 booking 4–6 months out, "Owner's voice, not corporate."

The five runs *without* Impeccable converged on one template: full-bleed golden-hour photo, light serif headline with italic emphasis, small top nav, one terracotta CTA. The three *with* Impeccable each built one concrete artifact from the guests' world and ran it through every section:
- a nautical chart / pilot book
- an altitude chart with an elevation rail
- a kitchen chalkboard

Copy, booking and craft details were very disciplined in all three. The copy in the without-runs was also good, so the visible gap was concept, structural interaction, truth discipline and browser-surface craft, not prose quality.

**Shared craft traits across the three Build-directly "with" runs (from HTML inspection)**
- All three define a full `:root` token set (named colour roles such as `--ink`, `--sea-*`, `--land`, plus `--ease: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)` and fluid gutters). Control run 1 has `:focus-visible` and tabular-nums but no selection, scrollbar or caret theming. — HTML of [e41a](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/e41a625c131eca65/index.html), [eac7](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/eac7aa5710b8a35e/index.html), [d009](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/d009bd6eb6399b7a/index.html), [control 2ebf](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/2ebfbb7b9aea4568/index.html)
- Each "with" run uses a semantic section structure (`nav aria-label`, `section aria-labelledby`, h1 → h2 → h3), a skip link ("Skip to content" / "Skip to the page"), 5–6 real raster images (`.webp`) with numbered captions, and 6–17 inline SVGs for drawn elements (chart, rail, scale bars). — same HTML files
- Navigation labels are human, not generic: "The family · Rooms · The table · When to come · Getting here" / "Who we are · Rooms · The table · The path · The cove · Practical things" / "Your month · The house · Rooms · The family · Dinner · To the sea · Getting here". The CTA is phrased as writing a letter ("Write to us", "Ask about your dates"), not "Book now." — same HTML files
- Colour restraint: each with-run has one committed ground (paper/sea, land ochre, or slate), one ink, and **one hot accent** (magenta or ochre) used only on the primary action and key map markers. — screenshots and tokens above

### Inferences
- What makes these look "shipped":
  1. One governing artifact from the audience's world, carried through nav, sections, tables and form, not just the hero.
  2. A signature interaction that answers the buyer's real question (which month, which room faces the sunset).
  3. Dense *specific* facts (metres, bearings, step counts, m², named rooms, people's names and roles).
  4. Honest limits and labelled placeholders.
  5. A low-pressure, personal enquiry flow with clear reassurance and a written success state.
  6. Themed browser surfaces and accessibility details.
- The italic-serif cliché still appears in the Impeccable runs (e41a sub-line, the design-first quote). The tool limits it rather than forbidding it, and the slop list treats it as a default, not a ban.

### Gaps
- I inspected the first-viewport screenshots and the HTML/CSS. I did not record scroll motion behaviour in a live browser, so motion is described only from code markers (reduced-motion, IntersectionObserver, ease token), not observed.
- Runs 4–5 of the control group and the design-first alternative comps (alternative-1/2) were not inspected in detail.

## Q3. Which traits transfer to a premium housing-estate site (3 house types, 18 plots, master plan, walkthrough)?

### Takeaway
Almost everything transfers, because the estate brief is structurally the same as the hotel brief. A small owner-run place with a fixed, nameable inventory (14 rooms vs 18 plots / 3 types) sells to a considered, high-ticket audience who decides on orientation, light, access and honesty. The hotel examples' best moves map one-to-one onto plot orientation, sun study, master plan as a drawn chart, an honest "what you won't find" list, and an enquiry framed as a personal letter, with every illustrative image labelled.

### Cited Findings (the source traits being mapped)
- Inventory listed with orientation, size and position ("where each one is, which way it faces, and how big it is"; "faces 270° · due west"), plus a table with Nº / Where / Faces / m². — [e41a HTML](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/e41a625c131eca65/index.html)
- A map/chart as the hero, not a stock photo, with a scale bar, compass and the property marked. — [e41a screenshot/HTML](https://impeccable.style/gallery/view/e41a625c131eca65/)
- A scroll-as-journey device (altitude rail, sections at real heights). — [eac7 HTML](https://impeccable.style/gallery/examples/eac7aa5710b8a35e/index.html)

### Inferences (mapping to the estate site)
- **Master plan = the chart.** Draw the site plan as the hero artifact, with north arrow, scale bar, plot numbers, road and green areas, and the sun path. Mark plot status with *one* accent colour. This is the estate's equivalent of e41a's nautical chart (the drawn plan is real geometry, not decoration, which passes the Consequence gate).
- **Plot list = the pilot-book room list.** Each of the 18 plots gets: number, house type, land area / usable area, facing (bearing plus words, e.g., "faces 90° · east, morning sun"), position ("corner, backs onto the green"), status, and one honest line ("closest to the gate; you'll hear cars at 7 am"). Show it as a real table with tabular numerals.
- **Three house types = the named rooms.** Give each type a name and a "turn it over for the facts" pattern (photo/render on one face, spec on the other), plus "what I'd tell a friend before they bought it."
- **Daylight / season study = "Your month."** Pick a month or time of day and the plan or house shows sun and shade. This answers the buyer's real question for a long-horizon purchase.
- **Walkthrough = the elevation rail idea.** A scroll-as-journey through gate → street → house → garden, with a persistent wayfinding rail. Keep motion as one orchestrated moment, with reduced-motion support.
- **Honesty block.** "What you won't find here" (e.g., no clubhouse pool, no 24-h shuttle). Disclose renders, sample photos and off-plan imagery as illustrative in captions and footer. Never invent prices, completion dates, availability, bank-loan approvals or customer counts unless confirmed.
- **Enquiry as a personal letter from the owner/developer.** Short form (names, contact, which type or plot or "choose for us", timing window), a response-time promise, and "nothing is reserved or charged until we've talked." The success state should cover the failure path (no reply → call).
- **Navigation in the buyer's words** ("The plots", "Three houses", "Where the sun falls", "Getting here", "Talk to us"), not "Features / Amenities / Contact".
- **Colour.** One committed ground drawn from the site's physical world, one ink and one accent reserved for the primary action and plot status. Choose light or dark from the buyer's real scene, not the category. Avoid the default cream + serif + terracotta "luxury" look unless deliberately earned.

## Q4. What anti-patterns / slop lists does impeccable name?

### Takeaway
The public `/slop` catalogue lists about 67 detectable anti-patterns in nine groups. The skill's craft floor adds a "Refuse" list and a hard ban on eyebrow/kicker labels. The calibration paragraph names the three convergent "AI looks" and the model's own cream-serif-lamplight bias.

### Cited Findings
- **Your design system (4).** Font, colour, radius or font size outside DESIGN.md. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Visual details (8).** Decorative grid-line background; border accent on rounded element; glassmorphism everywhere; side-tab accent border; hairline border with wide shadow; repeating-gradient stripes; extreme border-radius on cards; rough SVG illustrations ("A hastily drawn mascot or scene can make a finished page feel unfinished"). — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Typography (11).** Label above a heading; tiny interface text; flat type hierarchy; icon tile stacked above heading; italic serif display headline ("familiar shortcut to an editorial look"); badge above the main headline; oversized hero headline; crushed letter spacing; overused font (e.g., Inter); single font for everything; all-caps body text. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Color & contrast (7).** Radial-gradient background halo; soft spotlight behind content; AI color palette ("Purple gradients and bright cyan on dark"); dark mode with glowing accents; gradient text; gray text on colored background; cream/beige palette ("a default substitute for a considered palette"). — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Layout & space (12).** Tiny numbered section labels; cards flush against scroller edge; text covered by another element; unbalanced opening columns; heading closer to the previous section; hero metric layout; identical card grids ("give every point the same weight"); monotonous spacing; nested cards; line length too long (use 65–75 characters); content overflowing its container; clipped menus and popovers. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Motion (6).** Pulsing status dot; decorative blinking cursor; auto-scrolling marquee; bounce or elastic easing; animation that changes layout (use transforms); images that move on hover. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Copy (5).** Same text repeated in one container; em-dash overuse ("A dash in every sentence is a familiar AI writing habit"); generic marketing claims ("supercharge"); forced contrast (slogan-style "not X, but Y" lines); calling things "theater". — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Imagery (4).** Placeholder-style illustrations; jagged image masks; images hidden under overlays; broken or placeholder image. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **General quality (10).** JS errors on load; content stuck waiting to appear (entrance animation fails, so make content visible by default); cramped padding; body text touching the page edge; justified text; low-contrast text (WCAG AA 4.5:1 / 3:1); skipped heading level; tight line height (~1.5×); tiny body text (~16px); wide letter spacing on body text. — [Slop](https://impeccable.style/slop)
- **Craft-floor "Refuse" list (skill).** Same-size icon+heading+text card grids as page structure ("nested cards are always wrong"); hero-metric template; **kicker/eyebrow above a heading, which is "a ban, not a default: no brief earns it back"**; section numbers 01/02/03 without real sequence; needless modals; gradient text; decorative glass/blur; coloured `border-left/right` >1px; hard offset shadows outside neobrutalism; sparklines/rings/rounded rectangles standing in for content; monospace as a "technical" costume; system display faces (Impact, Arial Black); unicode/emoji as icons; geometric masks for organic contours; light/dark picked by category. — local `reference/craft-floor.md`
- **Calibration / convergent looks (skill).** (1) warm cream ground + high-contrast serif display + terracotta/signal-red accent; (2) near-black + one neon accent + glowing edges; (3) broadsheet-editorial hairlines + italic display serif + small tracked mono labels. Also the overused-font list (Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Syne, Space Grotesk, Space Mono, IBM Plex, Inter-as-display, DM Sans/Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans). — local `reference/new-work.md`
- **Finish-reviewer markers.** "Imitation material is the single most reliable mark of machine-made design" (CSS bevels, embossing, stamped metal, chalk); ground drift toward "warm cream on light grounds, blue-black slate on dark"; assets "applied at near-zero opacity or buried behind a wash" are "a compliance token." — local `agents/impeccable-finish-reviewer.md`
- A `detect` CLI and `/hooks` run these checks automatically after edits and in CI. — [Designing](https://impeccable.style/designing/) (links `/docs/hooks`, `/docs/detector`)

### Inferences
- For a premium real-estate site the highest-risk slop items are:
  - the cream/beige + serif + terracotta "luxury" default
  - italic serif display headline
  - eyebrow labels above headings
  - hero-metric strips ("18 plots · 3 types · 5 min to …" as big numbers)
  - identical amenity card grids with icon tiles
  - images zooming on hover
  - full-bleed photo under a dark overlay
  - em-dash-heavy copy and "not X, but Y" slogans
  - invented availability or prices
  - unthemed selection, scrollbar and focus states
